Current research

Observing polar ocean change.

I use satellite remote sensing, field observations, and coupled models to understand how the changing physical state of sea ice affects waves, ocean-surface forcing, and upper-ocean dynamics.

Multifrequency SAR observations reveal how C- and L-band measurements respond differently to evolving melt-season sea-ice conditions.

Theme 01 · Sea-ice observations

What can satellites tell us about the physical state of melting sea ice?

The challenge:

During summer melt, the surface conditions that matter most physically - melt ponds, wet snow, roughness, and evolving ice structure - also alter the microwave signal used to observe the ice.

My doctoral research used polarimetric C- and L-band SAR to show that the two frequencies contain complementary information about surface and volume scattering during advanced melt.

This provides a way to interpret sea-ice properties more physically under conditions where individual satellite measurements become ambiguous.

Theme 02 · Waves and upper-ocean mixing

Where can waves still influence mixing beneath Arctic sea ice?

The result:

Using coupled neXtSIM-WAVEWATCH III simulations from 2018-2022, I led a pan-Arctic assessment of the potential for Langmuir turbulence beneath sea ice.

The marginal ice zone emerges as a distinct and highly intermittent forcing environment.

Sea-ice concentration influences the average balance between wave- and shear-driven turbulence, but concentration alone cannot explain local mixing potential. Wave conditions and the evolving state of the ice also matter.

Theme 03 · A dynamic marginal ice zone

Why is the marginal ice zone more than a concentration threshold?

The physical picture:

The ice edge is not simply a boundary between open water and compact ice.

It is a transition zone where wave forcing, wind forcing, fragmentation, and changing ice cover create repeated shifts between upper-ocean mixing regimes.

This motivates my broader work: identify which satellite-observable properties of the ice and waves explain those transitions, and use those observations to evaluate how coupled models represent the ocean-ice interface.

Current NASA project · 2025-2028

When do ocean waves make satellite sea-ice concentration misleading?

The current question:

Passive-microwave sea-ice concentration products can disagree strongly in the marginal ice zone and during periods when surface and ice conditions evolve rapidly.

I am investigating how wave and ice conditions affect these retrievals and which physical mechanisms explain the observed biases.

The project combines complementary satellite observations with process understanding so that retrieval errors can be diagnosed by physical regime rather than treated as one uniform uncertainty.

Long-term direction

From satellite observations to better-constrained polar ocean models.

I am building an observational pathway from satellite measurements of sea-ice properties, to their influence on wave propagation and ocean-surface forcing, to better constrained representations of ocean-ice interactions in coupled climate models.
