The first time on sea ice

My first experience on sea ice changed how I understood the datasets I had been working with.

From a satellite image, the surface can appear organized into relatively clean regions. Standing on the ice reveals something very different: variability occurs over metres, centimetres, and sometimes even smaller scales.

Snow redistribution, deformation, small melt features, changes in roughness, and transitions between ice types can all occur within a footprint that a satellite sensor may represent as a single measurement.

That experience made uncertainty much more tangible and motivated my interest in combining observations at different spatial scales.
