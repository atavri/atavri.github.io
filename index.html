About

Connecting what satellites see with surface physics.

I study sea ice–ocean interactions using satellite remote sensing, field observations, and coupled models, with a particular focus on the marginal ice zone — where sea ice, waves, and open water meet.

My work spans C- and L-band SAR, satellite altimetry, and wave–ice modeling, from understanding melt-season sea-ice properties to investigating wave-driven upper-ocean mixing.

Across these projects, my goal is to connect what satellites observe with the physical processes shaping the polar oceans.

Research framework

From sea-ice properties to polar ocean dynamics.

01

Observe the changing physical state of sea ice

Use complementary SAR, altimetry, passive microwave observations, and field data to identify which physical sea-ice properties can be reliably observed across seasons and scales.

02

Understand how sea ice regulates waves and mixing

Investigate how ice concentration, dynamics, and seasonality modify wave propagation and the balance between wave-driven and shear-driven upper-ocean forcing.

03

Constrain coupled models with observations

Use satellite-derived ice properties and observed wave conditions to evaluate where model parameterizations capture — or miss — variability at the ocean-ice interface.

Langmuir turbulence in the Arctic Ocean

Using coupled neXtSIM-WAVEWATCH III simulations, we carried out a pan-Arctic assessment of the potential for Langmuir turbulence. The work highlights the seasonal marginal ice zone as an intermittent transition environment where wave and shear forcing coexist.
