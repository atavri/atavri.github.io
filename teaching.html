Teaching & mentoring

Learning through evidence, discussion, and independent questions.

I want students to understand the physics behind observations, recognize uncertainty and limitations, and become confident making their own evidence-based scientific decisions.

Teaching philosophy

From understanding measurements to asking better questions.

I teach remote sensing and Earth science as a process of connecting observations with physical interpretation. Students should leave the classroom knowing not only how to use a dataset, but also what that dataset can and cannot tell them about the physical world.

Remote sensing from principles to application

My teaching spans electromagnetic principles, microwave remote sensing, radar, altimetry, LiDAR, GIS, and environmental applications.

I connect sensor physics directly to real data analysis so that students understand why different observations respond to environmental properties in different ways.

Student-owned scientific questions

In advanced remote sensing, I have guided student projects on topics including wildfire monitoring, whale detection, and land-cover classification using SAR and optical imagery.

Students choose datasets, assess sensor limitations, evaluate evidence, and defend their interpretations rather than simply reproducing a prescribed workflow.

Inclusive rigor

Students enter Earth science with different levels of preparation in mathematics, physics, and programming.

I use visual explanations, scaffolded analytical exercises, clear expectations, and discussion while maintaining scientific rigor.

Questions and mistakes are treated as part of the learning process, not as evidence that someone does not belong in the field.

Mentoring independent researchers

I mentor Ph.D. and undergraduate researchers working with multi-sensor satellite observations, including SAR and ICESat-2.

My goal is to gradually shift ownership of the research toward the student as their technical confidence and scientific judgment grow.

I tailor the amount of structure, feedback, and independence to each researcher's experience.

02

Mentoring

Helping students become independent scientists.

Mentoring is an extension of my teaching. I work with students to refine research questions, navigate technical challenges, interpret uncertainty, and become increasingly confident making their own scientific decisions.

Undergraduate research

I have mentored undergraduate researchers using multi-sensor satellite observations, including SAR and ICESat-2.

Several former students have continued into graduate studies, and another undergraduate project is progressing toward a first-author manuscript.

Graduate mentoring

At Brown University, I currently mentor Ph.D. students through regular research meetings focused on technical problem solving, scientific reasoning, and research independence.

Career guidance

I value these conversations as much as formal classroom teaching.

Collaborative leadership
